Image-generation prompts for a Morg2SMILES hero image
=====================================================

For a wide illustration to sit at the top of the README or inside a blog
post. Not an icon -- the small-size legibility constraint that rejected half
the concepts in docs/logos/ does not apply here, so these can carry detail,
depth and atmosphere.

Written for Gemini / Imagen / similar. They are paste-ready: append the
shared style suffix, and set the negative prompt once.

Palette is inherited from docs/logos/_theme.txt so a hero image and an icon
can sit on the same page:

  slate   #334155    structure, backgrounds
  teal    #0d9488    the fingerprint side
  amber   #f59e0b    the molecule side, and exact matches

Practical notes
---------------

Aspect ratio
  3:1   banner above the README title
  2:1   image inside a blog post
  16:9  social preview / og:image

GitHub theme
  READMEs render on both light and dark backgrounds. Either commit to one
  and supply both variants via a <picture> block, or specify a mid-tone
  background that survives both.

Judging
  Generate four variants per prompt. Unlike the icon set, do not judge these
  shrunk down -- judge them at the width they will actually appear, which on
  GitHub is about 850 px.

Known failure modes
  Models ignore "no text" roughly a third of the time; regenerate rather
  than fighting it. They also treat hex codes as hue families, not exact
  values. Fingerprint-plus-molecule prompts pull hard toward DNA helices and
  glowing blue "AI" imagery -- the negative prompt below suppresses both.


Shared style suffix
-------------------

Append to any prompt below:

  Wide 3:1 composition, full-bleed background, rich detail, no text, no
  letters, no numbers, no labels, no watermark. Colour palette built from
  deep slate #334155, teal #0d9488 and warm amber #f59e0b, with amber
  reserved for the molecular structures so the eye reads them as the answer.


Negative prompt
---------------

  text, letters, words, labels, typography, watermark, signature, logo,
  icon, UI mockup, screenshot, chart axes, periodic table, laboratory
  glassware, scientist, person, hands, DNA double helix, brain, circuit
  board, glowing blue technology, artificial intelligence imagery


1. The many-to-one truth
------------------------

The project's most distinctive idea, and the one nobody illustrates. Folding
and bit collisions mean many molecules share a fingerprint, which is why the
task is generative rather than deterministic.

  An editorial science illustration. On the left, a single vertical strip of
  densely packed teal and slate rectangles, like one folded binary
  fingerprint, rendered crisply and solidly. From it, delicate amber lines
  fan out to the right into five or six different molecular skeleton
  diagrams, each a distinct arrangement of hexagonal rings and bond lines,
  all drawn in the same amber line weight. The composition makes clear that
  one pattern on the left corresponds to many different structures on the
  right. Clean technical drafting aesthetic, flat colour, fine linework,
  generous dark slate background.


2. Verified among candidates
----------------------------

The actual mechanism. recovery@20 means sampling k candidates and letting the
oracle pick, so this is what the model does, drawn literally. My pick of the
five: the only one that depicts the method rather than the inputs and outputs.

  An atmospheric illustration of a search resolving. A field of twenty faint,
  ghostly molecular skeleton diagrams floats in a deep slate space, drawn in
  thin translucent teal lines, overlapping and slightly out of focus. One
  single molecule among them is rendered crisply and fully opaque in bright
  warm amber, sharply in focus, as if selected and confirmed. A subtle amber
  glow separates it from the others. Depth of field, layered transparency,
  quiet and precise rather than dramatic.


3. The wall dissolving
----------------------

Large-scale and striking. The most conventional of the five, and the most
likely to produce something immediately usable.

  A wide cinematic illustration. The left two-thirds is a vast wall of small
  teal and slate squares in a dense binary grid, extending beyond the frame,
  flat and regular. Towards the right the grid begins to break apart: squares
  detach, drift, and reorganise into the bonds and rings of amber molecular
  skeleton structures, which become fully formed at the right edge. A clear
  transition from rigid uniform data to organic chemical structure across the
  width of the image. Flat vector illustration style, strong silhouette, deep
  slate background.


4. The fingerprint pun, rendered properly
-----------------------------------------

This failed as an icon because at 32 px it read as a wifi symbol. At banner
size the whorl has room to work.

  A large detailed illustration of a human thumbprint, its concentric ridge
  lines drawn in fine teal strokes against deep slate, filling most of the
  frame. Towards the centre of the whorl the ridges stop behaving like skin
  and become chemical bonds: straight amber line segments meeting at precise
  angles, forming hexagonal and pentagonal molecular rings nested inside the
  whorl. The handover from organic ridge curves to geometric molecular bonds
  is the focal point. Elegant, restrained, scientific-poster quality.


5. Risograph poster
-------------------

A different register entirely -- texture rather than diagram. Works well in
blog posts, less well as a repo banner.

  A two-colour risograph screenprint poster. Overlapping teal and amber ink
  layers with visible paper grain, slight misregistration at the edges, and
  mottled uneven ink coverage. The subject is a bold simplified molecular
  skeleton structure overlaid on a repeating pattern of filled and empty
  squares. Retro mid-century scientific printing aesthetic, limited palette,
  tactile and handmade, warm off-white paper ground.


Round 2: concepts 3 and 4, with the boredom taken out
------------------------------------------------------

Concepts 3 and 4 were judged the best ideas and the dullest outputs. The
fault is in the prompts above: "flat vector illustration style", "elegant,
restrained" and "quiet and precise rather than dramatic" are instructions to
be boring, and the shared suffix bans gradients and glow, which removes the
main source of drama. These replacements keep the two ideas and change the
register.

Use this style suffix instead of the shared one:

  Dramatic cinematic lighting with a clear light direction, strong rim light
  and deep shadow. High contrast, rich tonal range, volumetric haze. Dynamic
  asymmetric composition with a low or raking camera angle and a strong sense
  of scale. Shallow depth of field. Palette anchored in deep slate blue-grey
  and teal, with hot amber as the only light source in the frame.

And this negative prompt, which no longer bans the drama:

  text, letters, words, labels, typography, watermark, signature, flat
  illustration, vector art, clip art, infographic, diagram, symmetrical,
  centred, static, evenly lit, pastel, DNA double helix, periodic table,
  person, hands, circuit board


3a. The wall, monumental
------------------------

Scale is the cheapest route to excitement and the original had none.

  A colossal wall of dark slate and teal stone tiles, each tile etched with a
  simple binary mark, towering hundreds of metres and receding into fog. The
  wall is violently breaking apart at its right edge: enormous tiles tear
  free and hang suspended in mid-air, tumbling and rotating, trailing dust.
  As they fly outward they transform into vast glowing amber molecular
  lattices, hexagonal rings and bonds the size of buildings, burning hot
  against the cold grey stone. Dramatic low camera angle looking up along the
  face of the wall, raking side light, god rays through the dust, epic scale,
  cinematic.


3b. The wall, kinetic macro
---------------------------

Opposite strategy: get close and fast rather than big and slow.

  Extreme close-up of a disintegrating surface. In the foreground, sharply
  focused slate and teal tiles in a dense grid, their edges beginning to
  crack and lift. Mid-frame, tiles are already airborne, caught in motion
  blur as they spin toward the camera, some streaking past out of frame.
  Behind them, out of focus, the tiles have resolved into a glowing amber
  molecular structure, its bonds burning like filament, throwing warm light
  forward onto the tumbling debris. Shallow depth of field, strong motion,
  sparks and embers, high shutter-speed energy, dark background.


3c. The wall, shockwave
-----------------------

The wilder option: treat the transformation as a detonation front.

  A shockwave rushing outward across a vast dark plane tiled with teal and
  slate squares. At the leading edge of the wave the tiles are thrown upward
  in a curling crest, a breaking wave made of geometry, and beyond that crest
  the tiles have reorganised into an expanding web of incandescent amber
  molecular bonds racing outward like cracks of light. Seen from a dramatic
  three-quarter aerial angle. Enormous scale, glowing core, dust and debris
  lit from within, deep atmospheric perspective, cinematic science-fiction
  energy.


4a. The fingerprint as terrain
------------------------------

The strongest reframe of the two: stop drawing a thumbprint and make the
ridges landscape. Earns the drama honestly and the pun survives.

  An aerial view of a vast alien landscape whose ridges and valleys form the
  concentric whorl of an enormous fingerprint, carved into dark slate rock,
  each ridge hundreds of metres high and casting long hard shadows in low
  raking sunlight. At the centre of the whorl, where the ridges tighten, the
  rock gives way to an immense structure of glowing amber crystal beams
  meeting at precise hexagonal angles, a molecular lattice the size of a
  city, radiating warm light up the surrounding canyon walls. Dramatic low
  sun, long shadows, atmospheric haze, immense scale, cinematic aerial
  photography.


4b. The fingerprint, volumetric macro
-------------------------------------

  Extreme macro photograph of a fingerprint pressed against dark glass, its
  ridges rendered as towering walls of teal-lit texture receding into shallow
  focus, like canyon walls seen from the floor. From deep within the centre
  of the whorl, intense amber light pours upward and outward between the
  ridges in visible volumetric shafts, and silhouetted inside that light are
  the sharp straight bonds and hexagonal rings of a molecular structure,
  backlit so they read as hard black geometry against the glow. Dramatic
  chiaroscuro, heavy atmosphere, dust motes, very shallow depth of field.


4c. The fingerprint, shatter
----------------------------

  A fingerprint etched into a thick pane of dark glass, lit from behind in
  cold teal. The glass is shattering outward from the centre of the whorl:
  radial cracks, suspended shards hanging in space, each shard catching the
  light on its edge. Through the broken centre, blazing amber light reveals a
  complex molecular skeleton structure behind the glass, its bonds sharp and
  incandescent, as if the structure was always behind the print and has just
  been exposed. Dramatic backlight, lens flare across the shards, frozen
  mid-shatter, high contrast, cinematic.


Three dials, in the order worth turning
---------------------------------------

1. Scale. "hundreds of metres", "the size of a city", "towering". The round-1
   prompts specified no scale at all, which is why they came back as
   diagrams.
2. Light direction. "raking", "backlit", "lit from within", "low sun". Evenly
   lit images always read as flat.
3. Motion. "tumbling", "mid-shatter", "caught in motion blur". A static
   transformation is a diagram; a transformation in progress is an image.

If the results come back overcooked, dial down the lens-flare-and-embers
register first -- strip "sparks and embers" and "lens flare", keep the scale
and the light.

Expectation: 4a gives the best single image, 3a the best banner, since a 3:1
crop suits a wall far better than it suits a thumbprint.


Iterating
---------

The axis most worth varying first is register, not concept: the same idea
reads completely differently as technical drafting (1), atmospheric depth
(2), flat vector (3) or print texture (5). Run 2 and 1 first, since they
depict what the project does rather than what it takes in and puts out.

Prompts for a small square icon, as opposed to a hero image, are a separate
problem with different constraints -- see docs/logos/README.md for six
hand-drawn concepts and which of them survive at favicon size.
